# Let the Simulator Teach, Then Prove It

The project's RL fine-tuning has not failed for want of an anchor. It has failed because its per-decision learning signal is mostly noise, and the remedy is to replace that signal with exact simulator verdicts that are verified before any policy is trained on them. In the project's own measurements, only **6–29% of the variance in candidate scores comes from the candidate's own plan** (Y 0.06, T_narrow 0.08 after bias correction), 12–39% of candidate groups are fully tied, and a privileged value function with R² 0.64 ranked candidates no better than the noisy Monte Carlo score. Every fine-tuning run either stayed at the base or drifted away from it, losing T in proportion to drift while Y, D and L never moved beyond the noise of evaluating the same frozen policy twice. The signal that does clear the noise is the simulator used deterministically: score each base-sampled 8-control plan by its exact, follow-up-free effect under a disclosed privileged score, check that greedy best-of-N selection improves complete physical episodes (one Y smoke scene went from 0.671 to 0.909 final coverage), and only then distill those selections into the image policy with the ordinary denoising loss and a fixed-base anchor on every shape. No optimizer can promise "never worse"; that promise has to come from an exact fallback to the base plus one pre-registered, paired test on never-used scenes that ships the base unless every guard passes, which at the measured base rate needs about **1,000 scenes for T and T_narrow to certify a 5-point margin** and about 600 for Y, D and L. The recommended 60-hour plan spends about 5 hours on measurement, 8 on the oracle upper bound that decides go or no-go, 22 on oracle-to-policy distillation, 10 on retention and seeds, 6 on the final test, and holds 9 in reserve. The realistic prize is Y and T_narrow, whose failures are near misses at 0.81–0.94 peak coverage; D may gain coverage without successes, and **L (0/100 successes, 0.30 mean final coverage) will almost certainly stay unsolved**, which the plan treats as a measured bound to report rather than a failure to hide.

## Continuation noise, not forgetting, sank every fine-tuning run

The 100-scene test set first recalibrates what "the base" is. Under the deployed DDPM-100 sampler the frozen policy solves **T in 38/100 scenes (95% interval 0.29–0.48)**, T_narrow in 38/100, Y in 7/100 (0.03–0.14), and neither D nor L once; 1,000 separate value-data episodes agree at 40%, 38%, 6.5%, 0% and 0% ([project measurements](notes/project_measurements.md)). The 20 development scenes that every earlier run reported against showed T at 11/20, so they overstate the base by roughly 15 points, and any alarm threshold or power calculation calibrated at 0.55 needs redoing at about 0.39. The same table shows how chaotic the task is: the DDPM-100 and DDIM-32 samplers of the same network disagree on **38 of 100 T scenes** (24 succeed only under DDPM, 14 only under DDIM), so two nearly identical policies flip outcomes on more than a third of scenes ([project measurements](notes/project_measurements.md)).

| Base policy, 100 fresh test scenes | T | T_narrow | Y | D | L |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DDPM-100 successes | 38 | 38 | 7 | 0 | 0 |
| DDIM-10 successes | 35 | 34 | 6 | 0 | 0 |
| DDIM-32 successes | 28 | 32 | 6 | 0 | 0 |
| Mean final coverage (DDPM-100) | 0.65 | 0.66 | 0.49 | 0.32 | 0.30 |

Against that base, the long DiffusionNFT and AdvantageFlow runs trace a single curve. Runs that barely moved the policy (dense learning rate 3e-6, drift 0.007; LoRA 3e-4, drift 0.04) left every shape where it started. A dense AdvantageFlow run at 1e-5 reached drift 0.15 with T down to 0.20 on the 20 development scenes, and runs at 3e-5 and 1e-4 reached drift 0.6–2.2 and collapsed T to 0.00–0.15. **No run improved Y, D or L beyond the base-vs-base noise band**, and eight 1M-control LoRA runs at 1e-5 moved the policy by only about 1e-6 ([project measurements](notes/project_measurements.md)). Re-analysis shows that below drift 0.03 neither peak nor final coverage changes on any shape. Between 0.03 and 0.1, T loses near-threshold successes while peak coverage stays at 0.85–0.87, and real pushing skill erodes only above about 0.3. Two headline effects, a T_narrow "fall" and a D "rise" at near-zero drift, are mostly a lucky and an unlucky baseline ([independent diagnosis](notes/review_independent_diagnosis.md)). Drift grows against update count with a log-log slope of 0.6–1.2, the signature of steps in random directions rather than a consistent one ([red-team review](notes/review_red_team.md)).

The cause is arithmetic, not a bug. Each candidate's follow-up plans come from its own random stream, so candidates differ by independent continuation noise as well as by their plans. After correcting the score diagnostic's upward bias, the plan explains about 25% of score variance on T, 8% on T_narrow, 6% on Y and 29% on D, and a single draw has split-half reliability of 0.14–0.29 ([independent diagnosis](notes/review_independent_diagnosis.md)). The candidate controls only 8 of the 32 scored controls. The coverage shaping cancels inside a group, so the score reduces to coverage after 32 controls. Successes inside the scoring window occur for 0–4.5% of candidates, while the failures worth fixing stall at 0.92–0.94 peak coverage, which this score barely tells apart from success ([independent diagnosis](notes/review_independent_diagnosis.md)). Adam then moves each weight at a roughly fixed rate whatever the gradient's information content. Gradient norms of 0.006–0.37 never reached the clip of 1. Only about four optimizer steps followed each hard copy of the rollout policy, so the NFT and AdvantageFlow trust-region terms barely acted. The T-retention term sat at about **1e-4 of the main loss** and covered T alone ([independent diagnosis](notes/review_independent_diagnosis.md); [project measurements](notes/project_measurements.md)). DiffusionNFT's own authors warn that a strictly on-policy old model (η = 0, which is what `rollout_ema = 0` implements) "is prone to severe instability" ([DiffusionNFT](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16117)).

The obvious repair, a learned value function in place of the noisy follow-ups, has already been tried and did not help. A privileged state-value network trained on 800 episodes reached R² 0.64 on held-out scenes and correlated 0.73–0.93 with single-rollout returns. Yet in the candidate-ranking test its per-context correlation with the averaged truth swung from −0.9 to +0.9 and was not consistently better than the current score. In D contexts the candidates often ended identical, and within-candidate draw spread exceeded the between-candidate truth spread in most contexts ([project measurements](notes/project_measurements.md)). The red team predicted exactly this. Because the reward is a difference of coverages, adding γ⁸V(s₈) cancels the candidate's own coverage gain and leaves only the base policy's discounted future success, which is near zero on D and L. A useful ranker must also get the *differences* between sibling candidates right to within about **0.005–0.015 score units**, a precision that global R² says nothing about ([red-team review](notes/review_red_team.md)). The test was small: 3–5 contexts per shape, scored against a "truth" that is itself an average of only 8 noisy returns. It is therefore inconclusive rather than decisive, but it gives no reason to spend more hours on value-scored candidates. Here the literature and the project disagree most sharply. Chunk-level critics drive DPPO and Q-chunking ([DPPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.00588v3); [Q-chunking](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07969)), but those tasks do not combine a sub-percent decision effect with outcomes that diverge within 30–150 controls. Offline-evaluation benchmarks already show that an estimator's absolute error and its ranking quality can disagree ([Fu et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.16596)).

## Exact, follow-up-free simulator verdicts are the signal that clears the noise

The noise lives in the random continuation, not in the plan's immediate effect. Restoring a context and executing one candidate's eight controls is deterministic for fixed actions, a property the batched lanes must be checked to reproduce. The outcome after those controls therefore carries no continuation noise at all: every difference between sibling candidates is caused by the plans. The price is myopia, and the score must be built so that myopia is tolerable for near-miss shapes. Coverage alone is flat whenever the object is untouched or off the goal, which ties 8–21% of groups even when the object moved. Adding a privileged pose-error term (position plus angle) cut tied groups offline from about 39% to 18% on T_narrow and from 21% to 9% on Y, at zero simulator cost ([independent diagnosis](notes/review_independent_diagnosis.md)). The recommended score is a success bonus plus coverage plus a symmetry-aware pose-error potential. It is computed from simulator state and must be disclosed as privileged supervision under the project rules ([AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md)).

Myopic scores can be wrong, so the plan validates the score where it matters: in complete physical episodes. A simulator-oracle best-of-N episode samples N plans from the frozen base at every decision, scores each with the follow-up-free rule on restored copies, and executes the winner. The executed episode is never restored, so its outcome is physically real even though its choices used privileged lookahead. One Y smoke scene rose from **0.671 to 0.909 final coverage at N = 4**, still short of the 0.95 success threshold, at about 6.5 minutes per episode ([project measurements](notes/project_measurements.md)). The literature points the same way, but only when the scorer is exact. As read from GPC's tables, ranking with the true simulator lifted state PushT from 0.812 to 0.952 IoU, whereas V-GPS's learned critic scored 0.620 against the plain policy's 0.642 on vision PushT ([GPC](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00622v4)). The oracle also answers the red team's main objection to every reweighting or steering method, that none can reach behavior absent from the base's proposals. It measures directly how far recombining base proposals across about 37 decisions per episode can go ([red-team review](notes/review_red_team.md)).

If the oracle helps, the learning rule should copy it rather than estimate gradients from it. Expert iteration (filtered self-imitation) fine-tunes the image actor with Diffusion Policy's own denoising loss on the oracle's chosen plans. It keeps the loss on all 16 positions because the U-Net couples them, mixes in base samples at T and T_narrow states as a fixed anchor, and then repeats the oracle on states the updated student visits. Three results support this. PA-RL, which combines best-of-N selection, local Q refinement and distillation with the DDPM loss, reached an aggregate 711 against DPPO's 442, and removing its global best-of-N step dropped antmaze-large-diverse from 73→93 to 0→0 ([PA-RL](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06685)). RL-100 retrains by imitation on its growing dataset each iteration, and on a different, real-robot Push-T insertion variant it reports a rise from 40% to 100% ([RL-100](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14830v1)). Park et al. find that the policy-extraction step often matters more than the value objective ([Park et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09329)). Distillation also drops AdvantageFlow's negative branch, whose curvature falls to 0.01 for the worst candidate: an almost unbounded push to "make bad samples harder to denoise" ([independent diagnosis](notes/review_independent_diagnosis.md)). The teacher is machine-generated from the base's own samples, so no human demonstration or success label enters, but it uses privileged simulator lookahead and must be described as such.

The deterministic score also makes the cheapest progress check possible. After one distillation round, sample 16 plans from the student and 16 from the base at each of 64 held-out contexts with shared noise, and compare their mean follow-up-free scores. Each score is exact, so the only noise is plan sampling, and a student that does not move its samples toward the oracle's choices can be stopped within minutes ([red-team review](notes/review_red_team.md)). A matched control that distills uniformly random base samples instead of the winners isolates any damage caused by the training machinery itself. If any longer-horizon scored learning survives, the diagnosis' hygiene fixes still apply. Sibling candidates should share follow-up random streams, each update should use at least 64 contexts drawn near the goal or in contact, and a split-half gradient-cosine check should pass before any long run ([independent diagnosis](notes/review_independent_diagnosis.md)).

The frozen-base alternatives remain reserves, not first choices. DSRL steers the starting noise of a deterministic sampler and reported real-robot gains such as 2/10 → 9/10 within 3,500 steps ([DSRL](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15799)). The red team showed that under DDPM-100 the starting noise contributes about 3% of the first denoising step. The project's measurement that **DDIM-10 matches DDPM-100 within noise** (35 vs 38 on T, 6 vs 7 on Y) removes that obstacle ([red-team review](notes/review_red_team.md); [project measurements](notes/project_measurements.md)). But DSRL and residual RL (ResiP 5–54% → 88–99%) both train a critic that must rank actions at the same tiny within-context scale that defeated the value function, and ResFiT's authors state that the residual "cannot discover fundamentally different strategies" ([ResiP](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16677); [ResFiT](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19301v2)). Gradient guidance gave no gain in ITPS's kitchen task and 0–10 points in PPGuide, which also needs base successes to label its own data ([ITPS](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.16627v2); [PPGuide](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10980)). DPPO's pixel recipe, the only published RL success with a pixel diffusion policy, needs a likelihood-defining DDIM chain and many passes of the 251M-parameter U-Net ([DPPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.00588v3)).

## No-regression comes from an exact fallback and one pre-registered test

Chaos dictates what "never worse" can mean. Two nearly identical policies disagree on 29–38% of scenes: 26 T successes were lost and 26 gained across nine near-zero-drift evaluations, and 38 of 100 differed between two samplers ([independent diagnosis](notes/review_independent_diagnosis.md); [project measurements](notes/project_measurements.md)). No policy that differs from the base can therefore be never-worse scene by scene. The claim that can be certified is distributional: with 95% confidence, the shipped policy is not worse than the base by more than a stated margin on each guarded shape, and otherwise the base ships. That is the high-confidence policy-improvement template. It selects one candidate on training data, runs a single safety test on held-out data, and returns "no solution found" when the bound fails ([HCPI](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v37/thomas15.pdf)).

Three structural safeguards keep that fallback exact and cheap. First, the anchor must be fixed to the base, not to the previous iterate. PPO clipping, TRPO's KL constraint and DiffusionNFT's moving-average old model all bound change per update, so undirected steps accumulate ([safeguards note](notes/no_regression_safeguards.md)). Retention recipes that work use base-referenced terms far stronger than the project's 1e-4 ratio. InstructGPT mixed in pretraining gradients at γ = 27.8 ([InstructGPT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.02155)). Behavior cloning on stored states was the best retention method in Meta-World's RoboticSequence, with a coefficient of 2.0 in the same study's NetHack runs ([Wołczyk et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.02868)). The anchor should distill base samples on states from every shape, a proxy for forward KL to the base. That quantity predicted forgetting with R² 0.96 in RL's Razor's controlled toy study and 0.71 for language models ([RL's Razor](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.04259)). A target-KL controller should set the anchor weight, because a fixed coefficient yields different KL across seeds ([Ziegler et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.08593)). Second, the adapter should be LoRA, which "learns less and forgets less" ([Biderman et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09673)). It should be extended to the FiLM conditioning layers so that changes can depend on what the camera sees, and the rank-2 output layer's 4× scale quirk should be fixed ([independent diagnosis](notes/review_independent_diagnosis.md)). Scaling the adapter by s ∈ [0, 1] then interpolates exactly back to the base at s = 0. This WiSE-FT-style knob is untested on diffusion policies and must be chosen on validation scenes ([WiSE-FT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01903)). Third, a shape gate that reads the letter from the image and routes T to the untouched base weights makes T retention exact by construction. It weakens the "one shared set of weights" goal, so the team should record in `docs/DECISIONS.md` whether the gate counts as the shared policy or as a disclosed safety variant, and test both.

During training, rollouts are too noisy to catch slow damage, so monitoring has two layers. Every update logs the anchor loss per denoising-step bucket on fixed T and target contexts; this is the cheap, low-noise early warning. Every round also runs 60 T and 60 T_narrow episodes on a fixed monitor pool. At the measured base rate of 0.39, an alarm at **16 or fewer successes out of 60** has a 3.2% false-alarm rate. It catches a true drop to 0.19 with 95% probability and a drop to 0.24 with 74%, but a drop to 0.29 with only 41% (exact binomial, computed for this report). Two consecutive alarms, or anchor loss above twice its target, roll training back to the last passing checkpoint. Slow 10-point slides are invisible at this size and are caught only by the final test.

The acceptance test needs four disjoint scene pools, separated by seed range for every shape, because matched resets give all shapes the same initial pose for a given seed ([red-team review](notes/review_red_team.md)).

| Pool | Seeds (proposed) | Use | Reported? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Development | 100000–100019 | Debugging only | Never |
| Monitor | A fresh block, e.g. 400000+20i, i < 60 | Retention alarms during training | Never |
| Validation | 300000+20i, i < 200 (first 100 already inspected) | Choosing checkpoints, anchor weight and s, ranked by lower confidence bound | Labeled as used for selection |
| Test | A never-used block, e.g. 900000+20i, i < 1,000, checked against every range used to date | One confirmatory run | Final result |

Validation must rank candidates by a lower confidence bound computed as if from the test size. Picking the best of 10 checkpoints that all have true success 0.55 inflates the winner's observed score by about 0.08 even on 100 episodes ([safeguards note](notes/no_regression_safeguards.md); [HCPI](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v37/thomas15.pdf)). The test then uses many distinct scenes with one noise seed each, since repeated seeds on one scene add less information than new scenes. A second base seed on 300 scenes per shape lets the decision rule be run base-against-base as an A/A check, whose superiority arm should pass at most 5% of the time.

| Shape | Endpoint | Role | Test (one-sided α = 0.05, paired by scene and noise seed) | Scenes | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T | Success | Guard | Non-inferiority, margin 0.05 | 1,000 | ~940 pairs for 80% power at 38% disagreement |
| T_narrow | Success | Guard; secondary superiority | Non-inferiority, margin 0.05 | 1,000 | Same |
| Y | Success | Primary superiority | Exact McNemar | 600 | 0.07 → 0.12 detected with ~0.89 power |
| D | Mean final coverage | Guard; secondary superiority | Non-inferiority, margin 0.03 | 600 | About 0.03 resolvable if per-scene SD ≈ 0.3 |
| L | Mean final coverage | Guard; held-out transfer probe | Non-inferiority, margin 0.03 | 600 | Same |

Non-inferiority here means a test that the candidate is not worse than the base by more than the margin. The pair counts come from Connor's paired formula, which with no true difference reduces to n = (z₀.₉₅ + z₀.₈₀)²·p_d / margin², evaluated at the measured disagreement rate p_d ≈ 0.38 (computed for this report; formula via [MetricGate](https://metricgate.com/docs/sample-size-mcnemar-paired)). The Y power assumes independent outcomes within pairs, and the D/L coverage SD is an assumption that the A/A run must measure. A 10-point margin would need only about 235 pairs, but it would accept a policy truly at 28% against a 38% base, which no reader would call "never worse". The red team's simulation agrees in scale. At 300 scenes × 2 seeds a 5-point margin reaches only 0.61–0.77 power, and the 100-scene "all three must pass" rule would accept a truly better candidate only 20–35% of the time ([red-team review](notes/review_red_team.md)).

A candidate ships only if every guard passes and the primary endpoint passes after a Holm correction (an adjustment for testing several candidates) across at most two candidates. The guards together form an intersection-union test, which needs no further correction ([red-team review](notes/review_red_team.md)). The primary endpoint may move to T_narrow success or D coverage if validation evidence says that is where the gain lives, but only if the change is written into `docs/DECISIONS.md` before the test runs. The same entry must fix the sampler mode, batch composition and noise seeds, because batched and sequential sampling draw different random streams. Secondary measures are reported for every shape with Wilson intervals: success at 0.90 and 0.93 coverage, peak coverage, and time to 0.9. A claim that the *method* works, as opposed to *this checkpoint*, needs at least three training seeds ([red-team review](notes/review_red_team.md)).

## A gated 60-hour plan that front-loads the decisive experiment

The first hour should launch the already-implemented exact-replay oracle on 10 Y and 10 T_narrow validation scenes at N = 16 in six job slots, and the base-versus-base A/A run in the other two. The oracle is the one result that can kill or justify everything downstream, and evaluation is cheap: a 500-episode batched evaluation takes 10–14 minutes under load, and eight concurrent jobs is the measured throughput optimum ([project measurements](notes/project_measurements.md)). Meanwhile, CPU-side engineering should attack the 81–85% of training controls spent replaying prefixes. The options are physics-only replay that skips rendering and overlap computation until the last two controls, or an exact process fork, either one verified by checking that restored lanes reproduce identical rows for identical actions ([red-team review](notes/review_red_team.md); [independent diagnosis](notes/review_independent_diagnosis.md)). Hours below are wall-clock time on the H100 running eight jobs, and Phases A and B overlap at the start.

| 1 | **A. | 2 | **B. Oracle bound.** Greedy N = 16 oracle on 30 validation scenes × Y, T_narrow, D, L, T; a lookahead-24 variant with shared follow-up noise on 20 Y and 20 D scenes; a CPU sampling planner on L and D in parallel | 8 | **Gate B, per shape:** GO if paired mean final coverage rises ≥ 0.10 or success rises ≥ 15 points, with oracle-on-T not worse; if no shape passes, stop learning and write up the bound |

The oracle costs are estimates scaled from the smoke run. Greedy selection with exact replay needs about N × 5,600 replayed and scored controls per 37-decision episode, so N = 16 costs roughly four times the N = 4 smoke run: about 5 hours for 150 episodes across eight slots. The red team estimated the same workload at 3–4 hours with exact replay and about 1 hour with physics-only replay ([red-team review](notes/review_red_team.md)). Gate B's thresholds are the red team's own, declared before the result is seen ([red-team review](notes/review_red_team.md)). If Gate B fails everywhere, the remaining budget buys a clean negative result: the oracle bound, the shuffled-advantage null showing that the old trainer's drift carried no signal, the reliability measurements, and the CPU planner's verdict on whether L can be solved at all in 300 controls with this pusher. If the oracle helps only with lookahead, the signal needs a longer horizon with averaged, shared-noise follow-ups. That multiplies scoring cost by the number of follow-ups, and N and the scene count should shrink accordingly.

The plan also stops several lines of work. Further learning-rate or LoRA sweeps of DiffusionNFT and AdvantageFlow are exhausted, since every setting either stayed put or drifted ([project measurements](notes/project_measurements.md)). Value-scored candidates should not resume unless they are reformulated as V = −Φ + Ŵ with time-remaining inputs and shown to capture at least half of the oracle's selection gain ([red-team review](notes/review_red_team.md)). Stronger anchors alone produce the base everywhere. Best-of-N with a privileged critic is not an image-based actor and cannot be the deployed policy. Checkpoint choice on the 20 development scenes must end, because picking the best of 10 checkpoints there inflates a true 0.55 to about 0.72 ([safeguards note](notes/no_regression_safeguards.md)).

## L will almost certainly stay unsolved, and the report should say so

Every line of evidence says L is beyond this budget. The base never solves it (0 of 100 test scenes, 0 of 200 value-data episodes) and ends at 0.30 mean final coverage. No long run ever trained on it, so its flat curves say nothing about learning ([project measurements](notes/project_measurements.md); [independent diagnosis](notes/review_independent_diagnosis.md)). Frozen-base methods, best-of-N selection and anchored distillation can only recombine plans the base already proposes, and the residual and steering literature reports precision fixes from non-zero starting points, not new contact strategies ([ResFiT](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19301v2); [DSRL](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15799)). A myopic score also cannot credit the setup pushes that L likely needs, which pay off only many controls later. L should therefore stay a held-out shape whose mean final coverage is guarded for non-inferiority and reported as a weak transfer probe. It becomes a training shape only if the Gate B oracle lifts its coverage, and in that case its result is adaptation, not generalization. The CPU sampling planner is the honest way to say more. If it cannot solve L in 300 controls with privileged state, the shape is infeasible for this pusher and horizon; if it can, planner-to-policy distillation is a disclosed future step, not something to squeeze into the reserve.

D is the next least likely. Its candidate groups often end exactly tied because nothing touches the object, so even exact scoring is flat until a contact or approach term is added, which is a geometric prior that must be disclosed ([project measurements](notes/project_measurements.md); [independent diagnosis](notes/review_independent_diagnosis.md)). A D coverage gain is plausible; a D success is not. The best odds sit with Y and T_narrow. Their base failures peak at 0.81–0.94 and 0.92–0.94 coverage respectively, a fine-alignment problem of exactly the kind that selection and distillation can fix ([independent diagnosis](notes/review_independent_diagnosis.md)). Even there, the smoke oracle reached 0.909 without crossing 0.95. A credible end state is a few points of Y success (the test is powered to detect 7% → 12%), a T_narrow gain or tie, coverage gains on D, and T unchanged, all certified by one test. If the oracle cannot clear its gate, the project's strongest contribution is the measured explanation of why forward-process RL could not learn here, a useful negative result that the course explicitly accepts ([AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md)).

## Conclusion

The real constraint in this project is the ratio of what one decision changes to what chaos changes afterward, and the measurements put that ratio below what any learned critic in the literature has been shown to resolve. A restorable simulator offers a way around it that most published methods cannot use. Ask the physics, exactly and over a short horizon, which of the base's own proposals is better; prove in full episodes that following those answers helps; then turn RL into supervised learning toward a target that has already passed a physical test. That reframing also moves the safety argument. In a task where a third of outcomes flip by chance, no-regression is not something an anchor or trust region can give the optimizer. It is a property of the release procedure: an exact path back to the base plus a test large enough that "not worse" means within five points rather than within noise.

The oracle bound is also what makes the 60 hours worth spending whatever it shows. A positive gate yields a disclosed, privileged-teacher adaptation result with a certified no-regression claim. A negative gate yields the first quantified limit on how far a T-trained diffusion policy's own proposals can be recombined toward new letters. That is the evidence the project needs before asking a learned world model, whose contact predictions are not yet validated, to stand in for the simulator ([AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md)).
